
### Different gut microbiota in U.S. formula-fed infants consuming a meat vs. dairy-based complementary foods: A randomized controlled trial (Figure 1B)


**Results**

At 12 months of age, principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) based on Aitchison dissimilarity demonstrated a clear separation in gut microbial community structure between infants consuming meat-based versus dairy-based complementary diets. Individual samples clustered according to dietary group, with distinct group centroids observed along the first two principal coordinate axes. The 95% confidence ellipses indicated limited overlap between groups, suggesting consistent within-group similarity and between-group divergence.

Statistical analysis using PERMANOVA confirmed that beta diversity differed significantly between the two diet groups (P = 0.014). This indicates that the overall composition of the gut microbiota at 12 months was significantly influenced by the type of complementary protein consumed.

**Discussion**

The separation observed in PCoA space indicates that protein source (meat vs dairy), independent of total protein intake, exerts a measurable effect on the overall structure of the infant gut microbiome. Because Aitchison distance accounts for the compositional nature of microbiome data, these findings suggest robust differences in relative microbial abundances rather than isolated taxonomic changes.

The emergence of significant differences at 12 months implies that diet-induced microbial divergence develops over time, likely reflecting cumulative exposure to complementary foods during a critical window of microbiome maturation. This aligns with prior literature showing that the infant gut microbiome becomes increasingly responsive to dietary inputs during the transition from milk to solid foods.

Importantly, these differences in beta diversity may have functional implications. Distinct microbial community configurations are known to influence metabolic outputs, such as short-chain fatty acid production, which can affect host energy balance and growth. In the context of this study, the divergence in microbiota structure parallels observed differences in growth trajectories between groups, suggesting a potential mechanistic link between diet, microbiome composition, and growth outcomes.

Overall, Figure 1B supports the conclusion that the type of complementary protein introduced during late infancy is a key determinant of gut microbial community structure, with possible downstream effects on infant development and metabolic health.
