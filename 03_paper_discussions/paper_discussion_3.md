
### Inoculum microbiome composition impacts fatty acid product profile from cellulosic feedstock (Figure 4b)

In anaerobic sludge reactors, the microbial community showed strong cellulose-driven enrichment, with taxa such as _Clostridium lentocellum_ and _Bacteroides_ becoming dominant over time. These organisms are well-known for their ability to degrade cellulose and ferment resulting sugars into short-chain fatty acids, particularly acetic acid. This indicates a clear community shift toward fermentative bacteria, where microbes capable of efficiently utilizing cellulose outcompete others. Compared to other inocula, the enrichment pattern is relatively focused, suggesting that anaerobic sludge selects for a functionally efficient but less diverse fermentative consortium.

Furthermore, bison rumen reactors exhibited a more diverse enrichment pattern, with multiple taxa increasing in abundance rather than a few dominant ones. The community included a mixture of Cellulose degraders (e.g., _Clostridiales_), Fermentative bacteria (e.g., _Prevotella_). This reflects a functionally redundant and cooperative microbial network, where different organisms contribute to different steps of cellulose breakdown and fermentation. Such diversity suggests a balanced and versatile system, capable of producing a wider range of fatty acids due to the presence of multiple metabolic pathways.

In cattle rumen reactors, the microbial community became highly dominated by a small number of taxa, particularly _Prevotella ruminicola_ and _Fibrobacter succinogenes_, which together accounted for more than half of the total community. This indicates a **highly specialized system**, where a few highly efficient organisms drive most of the metabolic activity. These dominant taxa are known for:

- Strong **fiber (cellulose) degradation**
- Efficient **propionate production pathways**

As a result, the system shows **low diversity but high functional specialization**, which can lead to targeted production of specific fatty acids (e.g., propionic acid).
